29 Notation Cheat-Sheet

A one-page reference for the symbols used throughout the handbook. Each chapter defines terms at first use; this page collects them so a reader can look one up without backtracking. The running example is the HIV antiretroviral therapy cohort, so the plain-language column uses it where helpful.

29.1 Observed data

Symbol Name Plain meaning
\(W\) Baseline covariates What we know before treatment: age, sex, baseline CD4. The confounders we adjust for.
\(A\) Treatment or exposure Usually binary: \(A=1\) initiated HAART, \(A=0\) did not.
\(Y\) Outcome The event of interest, for example death within follow-up.
\(O\) One observation The full record for a patient, \(O=(W,A,Y)\) in the point-treatment case.
\(C\) Censoring indicator Marks a patient lost to follow-up before the outcome is observed.
\(T\) Event time Time to the outcome in survival settings.
\(n\) Sample size Number of patients (or person-visits) in the analysis.

29.2 Longitudinal and special-setting symbols

Symbol Name Plain meaning
\(L_t\) Time-varying covariate at visit \(t\) For example CD4 measured at each visit; both confounder and consequence of past treatment.
\(A_t\) Treatment at visit \(t\) Treatment decision at visit \(t\).
\(\bar A_t = (A_1,\dots,A_t)\) Treatment history All treatment decisions up to visit \(t\).
\(\bar a\) A treatment regime A full sequence of assigned treatments, for example “always treat” \(=(1,\dots,1)\).
\(d(W)\) Treatment rule A decision: given covariates \(W\), what treatment to assign (?sec-optimal-regimes).
\(M\) Mediator A variable on the pathway from treatment to outcome (Chapter 22).
\(Z\) Instrument Affects treatment but not the outcome except through treatment (Chapter 25).
\(S\) Study/sample indicator Marks trial versus target population in transportability (Section 25.4).

29.3 Potential outcomes and causal estimands

Symbol Name Plain meaning
\(Y^a\) or \(Y(a)\) Potential outcome The outcome a patient would have had under treatment value \(a\), possibly contrary to fact.
\(E[Y(1)-Y(0)]\) Average treatment effect (ATE) Average outcome difference if everyone were treated versus if no one were.
\(\tau(W)=E[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid W]\) Conditional average treatment effect (CATE) The ATE within a covariate profile: who benefits more (Chapter 20).
Risk difference / risk ratio / odds ratio Effect scales Absolute, relative, and odds contrasts of the two potential-outcome risks. The risk difference is the handbook default.
RMST Restricted mean survival time Average event-free time up to a horizon; a survival estimand (Chapter 23).
\(V(d)=E[Y^{d(W)}]\) Value of a rule Average outcome if everyone followed rule \(d\) (?sec-optimal-regimes).

29.4 Nuisance functions and the targeted-learning machinery

Symbol Name Plain meaning
\(Q(A,W)=E[Y\mid A,W]\) Outcome regression The model predicting the outcome from treatment and covariates. Also written \(\bar Q\).
\(g(W)=P(A=1\mid W)\) Propensity score The model predicting treatment from covariates.
\(Q^*\) Targeted outcome model The outcome model after TMLE’s update (fluctuation) step.
\(H\) Clever covariate The term that carries propensity information into the TMLE update (Chapter 8).
\(\epsilon\) Fluctuation parameter The size of the TMLE update; near zero when the initial fit already balanced treatment.
\(\Psi\), \(\hat\Psi\) Estimand mapping, estimate \(\Psi\) turns a data distribution into the target number; \(\hat\Psi\) is its sample estimate.
\(D(O)\) Efficient influence function How much one patient pulls the estimate; its spread gives the standard error (Chapter 8).
\(P_0\), \(P_n\) True and empirical distributions The unknown data-generating distribution, and the observed sample standing in for it.

29.5 Identification assumptions

Assumption Statement Plain meaning
Consistency \(Y = Y(a)\) when \(A=a\) The observed outcome equals the potential outcome for the treatment actually received; the intervention is well defined.
Exchangeability \(Y(a) \perp A \mid W\) No unmeasured confounding: within covariate strata, treatment is as good as randomized.
Positivity \(0 < g(W) < 1\) Every covariate profile has some chance of each treatment, so a comparison is possible (Chapter 12).

29.6 Estimators and tools, in one line each

Name One-line description
G-computation Model the outcome, predict it under each treatment, average the difference (Chapter 6).
IPTW Weight patients by the inverse of their treatment probability to mimic a trial (Chapter 7).
AIPW Combine the outcome and treatment models so either one being right suffices (Chapter 8).
TMLE A doubly robust substitution estimator that updates the outcome model and stays in the valid range (Chapter 8).
Super Learner A cross-validated ensemble that picks the best blend of candidate algorithms (Chapter 9).
HAL A flexible learner with a convergence guarantee targeted learning can rely on (Chapter 10).
L-TMLE TMLE extended to multiple time points with time-varying confounding (Chapter 14).
LMTP Longitudinal modified treatment policies: realistic, support-respecting interventions (Chapter 19).
